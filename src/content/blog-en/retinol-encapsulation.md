Retinol degrades rapidly when exposed to light, heat, and oxygen. The cosmetic industry has spent decades trying to solve this with liposomes, cyclodextrins, and encapsulated microspheres — with mixed results.

Electrospun nanofiber matrices offer a fundamentally different approach: the polymer shell physically separates retinol from environmental stressors while simultaneously controlling its release rate upon skin contact.

## Why fibers outperform liposomes

Liposomes are thermodynamically unstable — they fuse, leak, and degrade during product storage. A PVP-based nanofiber mat, by contrast, remains structurally stable at room temperature for **18+ months** in our lab studies, with retinol activity retention above 85%.

## The polymer choice matters

PVP and PVA both encapsulate retinol effectively, but they behave very differently on skin. PVP dissolves within seconds of contact with moisture; PVA requires mechanical disruption. This gives formulators a tool to tune release kinetics precisely.
